# Cover Letter Writing Guide

## Structure: 3-4 Paragraphs, 250-400 Words

### Paragraph 1: The Hook (3-4 sentences)
- Lead with a **specific achievement** or connection to the company
- Never start with "I am writing to apply for..."
- Reference the specific role and why it excites you
- Show you've researched the company

**Strong openings:**
- "When [Company] launched [product/feature], I immediately recognized the [technical challenge] — it's the same problem I solved at [Previous Company] by..."
- "After leading the migration of [X] to [Y] at [Company], reducing latency by 60%, I'm eager to bring that infrastructure expertise to [Target Company]'s growing platform."
- "[Company]'s commitment to [specific value/mission] resonates with my work on [relevant project]."

**Weak openings to avoid:**
- "I am writing to express my interest in..."
- "I saw your job posting on LinkedIn..."
- "Please consider my application for..."
- "To Whom It May Concern..."

### Paragraph 2-3: The Body (2-3 key points)
- Address **2-3 key requirements** from the job description
- Tell stories, don't repeat resume bullets
- Use the **STAR method**: Situation → Task → Action → Result
- Connect your experience to their specific needs
- Show impact with numbers

**Structure per point:**
```
At [Company], I [action] when [situation]. This [result with metric],
which directly relates to your need for [job requirement].
```

### Paragraph 4: The Close (2-3 sentences)
- Confident but not presumptuous
- Specific call to action
- Express enthusiasm without desperation

**Strong closes:**
- "I'd welcome the opportunity to discuss how my experience with [X] could accelerate [Company]'s [specific initiative]. I'm available at your convenience."
- "I'm excited about the possibility of contributing to [specific team/project]. I look forward to discussing how my background aligns with your team's goals."

**Weak closes:**
- "I hope to hear from you soon"
- "Thank you for your time and consideration"
- "Please don't hesitate to contact me"

## Tone Matching by Industry

### Technology / Startups
- Conversational but professional
- Technical specificity welcome
- Show passion for the product/technology
- OK to reference open source contributions, blog posts
- Slightly informal tone acceptable

### Finance / Banking
- Conservative, formal tone
- Emphasize quantitative results
- Reference regulatory awareness
- Avoid casual language
- "Dear Hiring Manager" preferred over "Hi team"

### Healthcare
- Professional, empathetic tone
- Reference compliance awareness (HIPAA, etc.)
- Patient outcome focus
- Certifications prominently mentioned

### Creative / Design
- More personal voice acceptable
- Portfolio references expected
- Show creative problem-solving
- Visual storytelling through words

## Key Principles

### Do
- Customize every letter for the specific role and company
- Mirror language from the job posting naturally
- Show you understand their challenges
- Include 1-2 metrics that demonstrate impact
- Keep it under 400 words (hiring managers spend 7 seconds scanning)
- Address to a specific person if possible (check LinkedIn)

### Don't
- Repeat your resume verbatim — tell new stories
- Use the same letter for multiple applications
- Apologize for gaps or lack of experience
- Use buzzwords without backing them up ("synergy", "leverage")
- Write more than one page
- Include salary expectations unless requested
- Use templates without significant customization

## When to Include a Cover Letter

- **77% of hiring managers** prefer candidates who submit one (Robert Half survey)
- Always include unless the posting explicitly says not to
- Even optional cover letters give you an advantage
- Exception: some tech companies (FAANG) explicitly don't want them

## Formatting

- Match resume formatting (font, header style)
- Date + addressee at top (optional in email applications)
- Your name + contact info (can reference "as shown in my resume")
- Sign off: "Best regards," or "Sincerely,"
- File format: PDF (unless specified otherwise)
