# Industry-Specific Resume Conventions

## Technology

### Format
- Skills section prominently placed (immediately after summary)
- GitHub, portfolio, and blog links welcome in header
- Technical project descriptions with specific tech stacks
- ATS optimization is critical — most tech companies use them

### Content Priorities
1. Programming languages and frameworks (list by proficiency)
2. Cloud platforms with specific services (AWS EC2, S3, Lambda — not just "AWS")
3. System design experience (scale, architecture patterns)
4. Open source contributions (if significant)
5. Certifications: AWS SAA/SAP, CKA, GCP Professional

### Language
- Use the exact technology names from the job posting
- "CI/CD" vs "continuous integration" — match their phrasing
- Include version numbers only for major framework differences (Python 3, React 18)
- Abbreviations are expected: REST, API, SQL, NoSQL, SaaS, IaC

### What to Emphasize
- Scale metrics: users, requests/second, data volume, uptime
- Migration stories: legacy → modern (with measurable improvement)
- Cross-functional collaboration with product/design
- Mentoring and technical leadership (for senior roles)

### What to Avoid
- Listing every technology you've ever touched
- "Expert in X" without evidence
- Listing languages without context ("Python" vs "Built ML pipeline in Python")

## Finance & Banking

### Format
- Conservative, clean layout
- No colors or creative formatting
- Formal section headings
- Chronological format strongly preferred

### Content Priorities
1. Quantitative results (revenue, P&L, risk reduction)
2. Regulatory compliance experience (SOX, Basel III, GDPR)
3. Financial modeling and analysis tools
4. Client relationship management
5. Professional certifications: CFA, CPA, FRM, Series 7/63

### Language
- Formal tone throughout
- Industry jargon expected (alpha, beta, AUM, derivatives, yield curve)
- Metrics in dollar amounts or percentages
- Risk management vocabulary

### What to Emphasize
- Revenue generation or cost reduction
- Risk mitigation and compliance
- Client portfolio size and growth
- Team size and P&L responsibility
- Regulatory audit experience

### What to Avoid
- Casual language
- Creative formatting
- Non-quantified achievements
- Missing certifications that are standard for the role

## Healthcare

### Format
- Clean, professional layout
- Licenses and certifications prominently displayed
- Continuing education section recommended
- Include license numbers if relevant

### Content Priorities
1. Clinical licenses and certifications (RN, MD, PA, NP)
2. Patient outcomes and quality metrics
3. Compliance experience (HIPAA, OSHA, Joint Commission)
4. EHR/EMR proficiency (Epic, Cerner, Meditech)
5. Continuing education and specialized training

### Language
- Patient-centered language
- Compliance and safety focus
- Evidence-based terminology
- Outcomes-driven metrics

### What to Emphasize
- Patient care quality improvements
- Safety record and incident reduction
- Interdisciplinary collaboration
- Population health outcomes
- Cost reduction while maintaining quality

### What to Avoid
- Specific patient information (HIPAA)
- Unsupported clinical claims
- Expired certifications
- Missing mandatory credentials

## Creative / Design / Marketing

### Format
- More visual freedom (but still ATS-readable)
- Portfolio URL is expected and essential
- Projects section with links to work samples
- Can include brief brand/style description

### Content Priorities
1. Portfolio with recent work examples
2. Tools proficiency (Figma, Sketch, Adobe CC, etc.)
3. Campaign results with metrics (engagement, conversion, ROI)
4. Brand development experience
5. Cross-functional collaboration (dev teams, stakeholders)

### Language
- More personal voice acceptable
- Creative problem-solving stories
- Brand-aware terminology
- Audience-focused metrics

### What to Emphasize
- Campaign performance metrics (CTR, conversion, engagement)
- Brand growth stories with before/after
- User research and testing methodology
- Creative process and iteration
- Cross-channel strategy

### What to Avoid
- Purely aesthetic portfolio without metrics
- Claims without supporting data
- Generic "creative thinker" without evidence
- Outdated tools or platforms

## Government / Federal

### Format
- Can be longer (federal resumes are often 3-5 pages)
- Include hours per week and supervisor info
- GS grade levels and salary history may be required
- Very detailed job descriptions expected

### Content Priorities
1. Specific duties and responsibilities (more detail than private sector)
2. Security clearance level
3. Veterans' preference status
4. Performance ratings
5. KSAs (Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities) addressed explicitly

### Language
- Formal, specific, detailed
- Match vacancy announcement language exactly
- Use government terminology
- Reference specific regulations, policies, directives

## Cross-Industry Rules

### Always
- Tailor every resume to the specific job posting
- Match the formality level of the industry
- Include industry-standard certifications
- Use metrics that matter to that industry
- Mirror the job posting's exact terminology

### Never
- Use the same resume for different industries
- Include irrelevant industry experience without translation
- Use jargon from a different industry
- Ignore industry-specific formatting expectations
- Over-design for industries that value substance over style
